\begin{abstract}
\noindent MANIFOLD treats a software artifact not as a bag of rules to match, but
as a \emph{mathematical object}: a typed, weighted graph enriched with
\emph{algebraic}, \emph{spectral}, \emph{topological} and \emph{geometric}
structure. Over this object we compute a scalar field of \textbf{vulnerability
potential} $V(x)$, which surfaces anomalous regions as geometric / topological /
spectral \emph{features} rather than as literal string matches. An
\textbf{autonomous LLM agent} navigates the resulting ``manifold'', formulates
hypotheses about those regions, and dispatches \textbf{formal verifiers} (SMT,
symbolic execution, abstract interpretation) that confirm or refute each
hypothesis --- closing the loop between statistical intuition and mathematical
proof. This document defines the intermediate representation, formalizes each
mathematical layer, states the mapping laws that connect mathematical features
to vulnerability classes, and specifies the agent architecture and roadmap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Traditional scanners (SAST/DAST) enumerate syntactic patterns. They are brittle:
they miss novel vulnerabilities and drown analysts in false positives. Two
capabilities are missing:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item A \emph{unified, quantitative representation} of ``program structure''
        onto which many \emph{independent} signals can be projected and fused.
  \item A \emph{reasoning loop} that converts ``suspicious region'' into
        ``proven vulnerability'' --- the LLM supplies the intuition, mathematics
        supplies the proof.
\end{enumerate}
MANIFOLD provides both.

A vulnerability is \emph{not} a pattern; it is a \emph{violation of a structural
invariant} (a broken naturality condition, a persistent cycle, a negative
curvature chokepoint, a taint flow across a spectral trust cut). Each layer
detects a different \emph{kind} of invariant violation, and the fused field
$V(x)$ ranks regions for the agent to inspect.

\section{The Intermediate Representation (IR)}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Definition 1 (Program graph).} A \emph{program graph} is a tuple
$G = (V, E, \tau_V, \tau_E, \omega)$ where
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item $V$ is a finite set of \emph{nodes} (modules, functions, blocks,
        statements, calls, variables, sources, sinks);
  \item $E \subseteq V \times V$ is a set of directed \emph{edges};
  \item $\tau_V : V \to \N_K$ assigns each node a \emph{kind} from
        $K = \{\text{module}, \allowbreak \text{function}, \allowbreak
        \text{block}, \allowbreak \text{statement}, \allowbreak \text{call},
        \allowbreak \text{assign}, \allowbreak \text{variable}, \allowbreak
        \text{parameter}, \allowbreak \text{source}, \allowbreak \text{sink},
        \allowbreak \text{gate}\}$;
  \item $\tau_E : E \to \mathcal{T}$ assigns each edge a \emph{type}
        $\mathcal{T} = \{\text{control}, \allowbreak \text{data}, \allowbreak
        \text{call}, \allowbreak \text{taint}, \allowbreak \text{trust},
        \allowbreak \text{auth}\}$;
  \item $\omega : E \to \R_{\ge 0}$ assigns each edge a non-negative
        \emph{weight} (default $1$).
\end{itemize}
\end{quote}
The IR is the single source of truth. Every adapter (source code, binary, web
API, LLM agent) must produce a program graph; every analysis consumes it. The
schema is language-agnostic and JSON-serializable (the Python and Rust layers
share the identical schema).

\section{Mathematical layers}
For each layer we fix a subgraph by edge type $\tau_E$, then compute structure.

\subsection{Spectral layer (algebraic graph theory)}
For an edge type $t$, define the symmetric \emph{adjacency} $A_t$ and
\emph{degree} $D_t$, and the \emph{combinatorial Laplacian}
\[
L_t = D_t - A_t .
\]
$L_t$ is positive semidefinite; its spectrum
$0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots$ encodes global structure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item $\lambda_2$ is the \emph{algebraic connectivity} (Fiedler value). Its
        eigenvector $f_2$ --- the \emph{Fiedler vector} --- induces a canonical
        cut $S^+ = \{v : f_2(v) \ge 0\}$.
  \item The \emph{spectral embedding}
        $\Phi^{(d)}(v) = (f_2(v), f_3(v), \dots, f_{d+1}(v))$ maps nodes into
        $\R^d$.
  \item The Cheeger inequality bounds the bottleneck ratio $h(G)$ by
        $\tfrac{\lambda_2}{2} \le h(G) \le \sqrt{2\,d_{\max}\lambda_2}$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Signal 1 (spectral anomaly).} The reconstruction error of a node under a
low-rank eigenmap reconstruction, and its distance from the Fiedler cut, are
used as anomaly scores.

\subsection{Topological layer (persistent homology)}
From the program graph we build a \emph{filtration} of simplicial complexes
(e.g.\ a Vietoris--Rips or flag complex over the shortest-path metric, or a
filtration driven by $\omega$). Persistent homology yields, for each dimension
$k$, a \emph{persistence diagram} $\mathrm{Dgm}_k$ of birth--death pairs.
$\beta_0$ counts components, $\beta_1$ counts independent cycles; points far
from the diagonal have high persistence. The \textbf{Mapper} algorithm
(Singh--M\'emoli--Carlsson) builds a navigable \emph{nerve} from a filter
function $f: V \to \R$, producing the map that gives the project its name.

\subsection{Geometric layer (discrete curvature)}
The \emph{Ollivier--Ricci curvature} of an edge $(u,v)$ is
\[
\kappa(u,v) = 1 - \frac{W_1(m_u, m_v)}{d(u,v)},
\]
with lazy random-walk measures $m_u, m_v$ and Wasserstein-1 distance $W_1$.
Negative curvature marks \emph{funnels} through which many shortest paths pass.
The \emph{Forman--Ricci} curvature (unweighted) is $\kappa_F(u,v) = 4 - \deg(u)
- \deg(v)$, cheaply flagging high-degree hubs.

\subsection{Algebraic layer (lattices \& category theory)}
\emph{Abstract interpretation.} The \emph{taint lattice}
$\mathbb{T} = \{\bot = \text{clean} \sqsubseteq \top = \text{tainted}\}$ (a
product lattice over tags) gives a \emph{Galois connection}
$(\alpha, \gamma)$ between the concrete collecting semantics and the abstract
taint domain, guaranteeing soundness. \emph{Category theory.} The program is a
category $\catProg$; effects are monads, authorization is a functor
$F : \catProg \to \catAuth$. \textbf{Design law (naturality of taint):} a
vulnerability is a taint flow that breaks the naturality square of $F$.

\subsection{Formal layer (symbolic execution \& model checking)}
Symbolic execution computes a path condition $\Phi$ and symbolic state $\sigma$;
an SMT solver checks $\mathrm{SAT}(\Phi \wedge \text{bad})$, whose model is a
concrete exploit. Reachability is the CTL formula $\mathbf{EF}\,\mathrm{bad}$;
taint propagation is a finite automaton whose accepted language is exactly the
set of source $\to$ sink paths.

\[
V(x) = \sigma\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_i \alpha_i \, s_i(x)\Big),
\]
where $s_i$ are normalized layer signals (spectral anomaly, persistence
outlier, curvature, lattice height, formal reachability), $\alpha_i$ are
weights, and $\sigma$ a logistic squeeze. $V$ is a ranking, a
Mapper filter, and a cost field for navigation. In the current prototype the
weights are \emph{uniform} ($\alpha_i = 1/N$ over the available signals) and
$V$ is reported as the raw fused score; calibrating $\alpha_i$ (balanced
initialization $\to$ empirical tuning on the micro-benchmarks $\to$ a logistic
classifier over labeled traces) is future work.

\section{A worked example (end-to-end)}
Consider this ten-line Python fragment:
\begin{verbatim}
def search(db, query_param):
    sql = "SELECT * FROM users WHERE name = '" + query_param + "'"
    cursor = db.cursor()
    cursor.execute(sql)
\end{verbatim}
The pipeline produces: (i) an IR with a \texttt{source} node
(\texttt{param:query\_param}), a \texttt{sink} node (\texttt{cursor.execute},
category \texttt{sql}) and a \texttt{taint} edge between them; (ii) the field
$V(x)$ spikes at the sink (taint + formal signals active); (iii) the Z3 symbolic
verifier returns \texttt{SAT} with a concrete witness (e.g.\
\texttt{"x' OR '1'='1"}), proving the sink's argument depends on
the attacker-controlled symbol. The parameterized variant
\texttt{execute(query, (param,))} is correctly \emph{not} flagged, and a
\texttt{@login\_required} gate on the enclosing function upgrades the finding to
an L4 naturality violation (a taint flow crossing an \texttt{auth} edge).

\section{The autonomous LLM agent}
A closed loop: (1) \emph{ingest} into the IR; (2) \emph{embed \& map} ($V(x)$,
spectral embedding, persistence, curvature, Mapper); (3) \emph{hypothesize}
(the LLM proposes candidates from the manifold); (4) \emph{verify} (the formal
layer proves or refutes); (5) \emph{refine} (findings re-weight and re-embed);
(6) \emph{report}. The LLM provider layer targets any OpenAI-compatible endpoint
via \textbf{LiteLLM}. The LLM does not do raw detection: mathematics proposes
regions, the LLM proposes hypotheses, the formal layer proposes proofs.

\section{Design challenges and mitigations}
A design review identified five structural risks. For each we state the
mitigation adopted in the prototype and the open problem.

\textbf{C1 -- The bottleneck paradox.} Negative curvature flags funnels, but
healthy software \emph{deliberately} concentrates flow through auth middleware,
sanitizers, dispatchers and loggers. Reporting these as privilege-escalation
leads yields systematic false positives. \emph{Mitigation:} the agent
discriminates defensive chokepoints (by role) and drops or down-ranks them; the
principled fix is to tag \texttt{trust}/\texttt{auth} edges (already reserved in
the IR) and treat a chokepoint as defensive iff it is the target of an
\texttt{auth} edge. \emph{Open:} learning the defensive/offensive label from data.

\textbf{C2 -- Symmetrization destroys causality.} The combinatorial Laplacian
$L=D-A$ and standard persistent homology act on undirected metric spaces, but
control and data flow are directed: $A$ tainting $B$ is not $B$ tainting $A$.
Symmetrizing can destroy the direction of taint. \emph{Mitigation:} the
prototype implements Chung's directed Laplacian restricted to the largest
strongly connected component; the Perron vector $\pi$ is computed by power
iteration with a small PageRank-style teleportation (damping $\alpha = 0.01$),
which keeps the stationary distribution well-posed on periodic chains. A DAG has
no SCC of size $\ge 2$, so the directed signal is deliberately \emph{undefined}
there --- which is itself a meaningful answer: acyclic flows carry no direction
to preserve and are handed to the lattice / reachability layers instead.
\emph{Open:} directed flag complexes / path homology
(Grigor'yan--Lin--Muranov--Yau).

\textbf{C3 -- Cost of optimal transport.} Ollivier--Ricci requires a
transportation LP per edge: $\mathcal{O}(|E|\cdot\text{LP})$, prohibitive beyond
$\sim 10^5$ edges. \emph{Mitigation:} Forman--Ricci
$\kappa_F = 4 - \deg(u) - \deg(v)$ is $\mathcal{O}(|E|)$ and is the default
chokepoint signal; entropy-regularized (Sinkhorn) transport is implemented as the
fast alternative to the exact LP. \emph{Open:} adaptive regularization
schedules for very large graphs.

\textbf{C4 -- Operationalizing L4 (naturality).} A constructive rule is
required. Let \texttt{auth} edges $E_A$ mark privilege boundaries. Define the
authorization functor $F$ to label a node by whether it is reachable \emph{behind}
an auth edge. Then a taint flow $s \to^{*} k$ \emph{violates naturality} iff its
path crosses at least one auth edge --- i.e. untrusted data enters a protected
region without the boundary neutralizing it. This is a reachability query over
the combined $(\text{data} \cup \text{taint} \cup \text{auth})$ graph, directly
computable and automatable. \emph{Implemented:} an authorization extractor emits
\texttt{gate} nodes and \texttt{auth} edges from privilege-gate decorators, and a
taint finding inside a gated function is reported as a naturality violation.

\textbf{C5 -- Manifold-to-verifier bridge.} Geometric features are
quantitative; Z3 needs exact preconditions. We fix a \emph{grammar}: a
hypothesis is the structured spec \texttt{(sink, source, line, category)}. The
manifold supplies the spec (it knows the sink and line), the LLM supplies the
interpretation (CWE, description, confidence), and the verifier consumes the
spec --- never the LLM prose. This keeps the SMT query precise and reproducible.

The underlying thesis stands: static analyzers fail because they match words
instead of invariants in a topological structure. The criterion is whether
$V(x)$ prunes the search space for Z3/angr more effectively than a plain DFS
over the call graph.

\section{Validation and responsible use}
Validation is two-stage. Stage 1 (complete) runs the toy micro-benchmarks of
F1--F7 against known ground truth: every seeded vulnerability is confirmed and
every sanitizer / parameterized-query / argv-form false positive is excluded.
Stage 2 (ongoing) measures precision/recall of the fused field versus raw taint
over labeled corpora (OWASP Benchmark, SARD); this is experimental validation at
scale, distinct from the completed prototype. The harness is implemented: a
labeled toy benchmark reports precision/recall/F1 for the raw taint pass, the
Z3-verified pass and a \emph{calibrated} field whose weights $\alpha_i$ are
learned by leave-one-out logistic regression over the cheap signals --- on the
toy corpus the calibrated field recovers the reentrancy cycle (topological
signal) that the taint pass misses, raising recall while keeping precision at
$1.0$. A corpus loader accepts a JSON manifest or a ``vulnerable|safe``
directory, so the harness can be pointed at OWASP Benchmark / SARD exports; and
the binary adapter has an optional angr backend (``CFGFast`` + interprocedural
taint) in addition to the zero-dependency objdump path. Running the Java SAST
adapter over the full OWASP Benchmark 1.2 (2740 test cases) gives the naive
baseline: precision $0.515$ / recall $0.426$ / F1 $0.466$ on the seven taint
categories, and precision $0.544$ / recall $0.634$ / F1 $0.586$ over all eleven
categories --- the reference point that the fused field, calibration and the
verifier are meant to improve on. MANIFOLD is a defensive /
authorized-testing tool; we release under the assumption of authorized use and
encourage coordinated disclosure.

\subsection{Empirical study on OWASP Benchmark 1.2}
We ran the Java SAST adapter over the full corpus and profiled the false
negatives. Three \emph{generalizable} gaps explained most of the loss, and were
fixed (none is tuned to the benchmark):

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Branch-aware taint.} A sequential, path-insensitive pass let a
        null guard (\texttt{if (param == null) param = "";}) clear the taint,
        even though the tainted value survives on the other path. We now
        snapshot both branches and \emph{join} their environments (union of
        tags), the taint analogue of a lattice least-upper-bound.
  \item \textbf{Receiver and state taint.} Sinks such as
        \texttt{statement.execute()} and \texttt{pb.start()} take no argument
        --- the taint lives in the \emph{receiver} object, which was obtained
        from a tainted constructor/mutator. We propagate taint from the
        receiver, and mark a variable tainted when a mutator
        (\texttt{add}/\texttt{put}/\texttt{command}/...) stores a tainted value
        into it.
  \item \textbf{Sink coverage.} The XSS family includes
        \texttt{printf}/\texttt{format}, and only writes to the
        \emph{HTTP response writer} are XSS --- \texttt{System.out.println} of
        tainted data is not. Restricting the XSS sinks to the response writer
        removed a large class of false positives.
\end{enumerate}

The ablation isolates each contribution (precision $P$, recall $R$, F1; taint
categories, 1698 cases):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
variant & $P$ & $R$ & F1 \\
\midrule
initial naive adapter & 0.515 & 0.426 & 0.466 \\
+ branch-join, receiver/state taint, sink coverage & 0.530 & 0.856 & 0.655 \\
+ XSS response-writer restriction (final) & 0.549 & 0.834 & 0.662 \\
\midrule
final, without branch-join & 0.515 & 0.555 & 0.535 \\
final, without XSS restriction & 0.530 & 0.856 & 0.655 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Branch-aware taint is the dominant recall lever ($+0.28$ recall); the
response-writer restriction is the precision lever. The final adapter reaches
$P=0.549$, $R=0.834$, $\mathrm{F1}=0.662$ on the taint categories and
$P=0.572$, $R=0.842$, $\mathrm{F1}=0.681$ over all eleven (crypto/hash are
configuration-driven and partly out of scope). The \emph{residual} errors are
the benchmark's deliberately adversarial ``false'' cases --- debatable
parameterized-query and \texttt{ProcessBuilder}-list cases --- and cross-category
distractor flows: precisely the precision that the topological/spectral signals,
the Z3 verifier and the calibrated field are designed to recover.
